Undergraduate Research

Behavioral Biometric Analysis for Task and Keystroke Inference

2025-2026 | University at Albany | CAFE Lab

An empirical study of motion-sensor side-channel attacks on smartwatches and a differential-privacy defense that balances privacy against legitimate sensing utility.

Project Overview

Smartwatch Sensor Privacy and Differential Privacy Defense

Role: Undergraduate Researcher

Conducted within the Cognitive Security Accountability, Fairness and Explainability (CAFE) Lab at the University at Albany under the same advisor as the EMBER + LLM explanation work.

Wearables continuously stream accelerometer and gyroscope data to support health and activity features, but the same signals can leak typing-related behavior to a malicious app. This project designs and evaluates a reproducible pipeline that (1) demonstrates the attack, (2) applies feature-level differential privacy with the IBM diffprivlib Laplace mechanism, and (3) quantifies the privacy-utility tradeoff at fixed privacy budgets.

The work is framed as a feasibility and defense study rather than an end-to-end keystroke-content recovery attack. It uses two public datasets independently: WISDM (smartwatch motion for typing-vs-non-typing task inference) and Keystroke100 (keystroke dynamics for closed-set user identification). The core research question is how much attacker accuracy is lost at a given privacy budget, and how much legitimate activity-recognition utility must be sacrificed in exchange.

Status: Manuscript drafted — not yet submitted to IEEE.

Methodology

Three-Stage Evaluation Pipeline

  • Stage 1 - Attack demonstration. Trained typing-vs-non-typing classifiers on WISDM smartwatch accelerometer and gyroscope features (182 numeric features per sample) with subject-disjoint train/validation/test splits, evaluating Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and SVM-RBF. In parallel, trained 100-class keystroke-dynamics identification models on Keystroke100 using 13 latency and pressure features.
  • Stage 2 - Differential privacy defense. Implemented feature-level Laplace perturbation with IBM diffprivlib. Per-feature clipping bounds are computed from the training split only and reused for validation and test. The per-feature Laplace scale is b_j = (U_j - L_j) / ε, with one fixed privacy budget per run.
  • Stage 3 - Privacy-utility evaluation. Measured attack accuracy degradation under both clean-trained and attacker-aware retraining, and measured utility loss on a non-typing activity-recognition task using a Random Forest utility model.
  • Parameter sweep. Evaluated ε values of 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 to characterize how fixed privacy budgets move the privacy-utility frontier.
  • Reproducibility. Fixed random seeds, public datasets only, and a documented pipeline so the benchmark can be reproduced and extended.

Why It Matters

Context and Impact

Prior work (MoLe, Maiti et al.) has shown that unrestricted wrist-motion streams can be used to infer typing activity and narrow candidate words. The standard defense - block sensor access while typing is detected - breaks legitimate uses like fall detection and health tracking. This project evaluates whether fixed-noise differential privacy can offer a middle path, and reports measured tradeoffs rather than claiming a universal defense.

The work was featured by CBS6 Albany and by the University at Albany news center as part of ongoing coverage of consumer wearable security risk.
